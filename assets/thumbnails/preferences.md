# Channel thumbnail style and guidelines

These principles apply across videos. Select the actual subject, hook, assets,
and expression from each video's evidence; do not reuse a previous video's
premise or treat candidate numbers as permanent editorial categories.

## Visual identity

- Large, recognizable ElmilloR reaction cutout, one clear game subject, and a
  big, short headline. Inspiration: Thebausffs and Drututt's bold, readable
  gaming-thumbnail approach, with ElmilloR's own identity.
- Keep the enlarged reaction and white silhouette outline; never outline straight
  photo-crop boundaries or extend them into dividing lines.
- Keep cinematic depth and purposeful lighting. Bold, not aggressively chaotic.
- Use stable, bold condensed lettering with restrained colored accents, clean
  outlines and modest depth. Keep a straight baseline and consistent letter shapes;
  express emphasis through word size and color, not playful, wobbly, bouncing,
  warped or individually rotated letters. Avoid curved baselines and chaotic type.
- Controlled texture and finish, not playful letterforms: a subtle game-themed
  gradient, shallow bevel and faint grain/worn texture are welcome. Keep letter
  interiors mostly solid and every word readable at feed size. No heavy distressing
  or cracks. Keep supporting words cleaner than the emphasized word.
- No headline underlines, red laser streaks, glowing highlight bars or light
  trails beneath/across the text.
- Allow at most one localized glow or sparse particle effect around an emphasized
  word when it reinforces the game subject. No stacked effects or particle showers;
  preserve readable text, clear silhouettes and faithful game designs.
- Other creators' photos are style references for Nano art direction only.
  Do not send them to the image generator: ElmilloR's original reaction is its
  sole human identity reference; never blend another creator's facial features.
- Use strong foreground/background separation and restrained supporting color.
  Contrast should guide attention rather than make every element equally loud.
- Source photos face left: on the right keep them unflipped; on the left mirror
  them to face right. Prefer right placement; always face inward.
- One reaction only: a single thin silhouette outline, no offset head, ghost copy
  or human-shaped shadow. Layer background → reaction → headline; text is topmost.

## Editorial judgment

- Never make a failed play, lost objective, misplay or other downer the title or
  thumbnail's hook, even when it is funny or the strongest spoken reaction.
- Prefer a genuinely outstanding positive play, unusual pick/role/build, strong
  performance or useful insight when the footage supports it. Do not inflate an
  ordinary moment into an amazing achievement just to manufacture a hook.
- When no positive standout warrants a moment-led thumbnail, use honest champion,
  role or session-led gameplay packaging. A recognizable champion, appealing
  reaction and simple headline are enough; no amazing play or curiosity gap is
  required. Champion nicknames and a night-session theme can work when grounded.
- A standard core item is not a build hook. Omit routine item mentions and icons
  unless they genuinely explain something distinctive. Fiddlesticks with
  Protobelt is ordinary; Fiddlesticks support with Zeke's can be a meaningful
  unusual-role/build hook when evidenced. Apply that distinction across champions.
- Match the reaction to the actual emotion. Excitement, skepticism, confusion,
  amusement, and shock are alternatives, not a fixed mapping from content type.
- A question is not hype. Pair questioning copy with curiosity or skepticism,
  never the `overly-hyped` reaction.
- Inspect the actual available reaction photos and their catalog descriptions.
  Explain the photo's fit to each concept's evidence, headline and emotional promise.
  Closed-eye clenched-fist excitement is not the default for builds or questions.
- Prefer `smiling` more often for positive, confident, playful and normally exciting
  hooks. The clenched-fist pose is named `overly-hyped` and is a rare exception for
  genuinely extreme hype, not an ordinary win or merely a strong play. Its selection
  must explain the exceptional evidence and why smiling would undersell the moment.
- Experiment discipline: no additional paid image generations, revisions or broad
  evidence ingestion without the user requesting the next run. A cash budget alone
  is not permission for repeated image experiments; agree on the call count too.
- Three candidates should test different compelling interpretations of this
  video, not synonyms or mandatory action/champion/build slots.
- Preserve factual grounding. Never invent achievements, outcomes, items,
  characters, or stakes to strengthen the packaging.

## Composition and assets

- Title, headline, game subject and reaction must deliver one cohesive promise.
  If the copy names a champion, that champion must be clearly visible and prominent.
  Use the official supplied artwork when source gameplay obscures the model;
  do not keep a champion-led headline over scenery with the champion missing.
  Supporting assets/effects must reinforce that same idea, not add competing hooks.
- Choose gameplay, a character close-up/artwork, or another relevant background
  according to the hook. A background must help explain the subject or mood.
- In gameplay, make the important action identifiable at small size. Prefer a
  useful focal point over a wide screen full of unrelated HUD and effects.
- Use supporting item, rune, or character icons only when they add information.
  Avoid repeating a character badge when the main artwork already identifies it.
- Up to 2-3 relevant item/rune icons may explain an unusual build or synergy;
  neither one icon nor a full inventory is mandatory. Standard core items are
  not useful decoration for ordinary champion-led gameplay packaging.
- If an evidenced item/rune explains the hook, show its exact icon; do not let
  generic champion artwork hide the build's defining detail.
- Every League champion, character, skin, spell, weapon, monster and other game
  element must closely follow a supplied visual reference, not the model's memory.
  Use official artwork/exact assets or clear source-gameplay details. Preserve
  silhouette, anatomy, proportions, colors, costume, equipment and spell shapes;
  cinematic lighting/composition is not permission to redesign them.
- If an element lacks a clear supplied reference, omit it or use a simpler
  referenced scene. Do not invent a monster, spell or half-visible body from a
  name, a tiny ambiguous screenshot or another creator's stylized thumbnail.
- Prefer removing the desktop microphone from a cleaned real-photo cutout when
  possible without changing the person. Keep the headset; never synthesize the face.
- Keep text and eyes unobstructed. Avoid accidental crops through facial features.
- Decorative shapes, outlines, and effects must improve hierarchy or separation;
  do not add them merely to make candidates look different.

## Copy and terminology

- Short, natural Spanish headlines; title and thumbnail should complement each
  other. Avoid literal sentence fragments and keyword soup.
- Name the champion/role/session or a genuine positive hook. Reject generic labels
  such as “ELECCIÓN INSÓLITA” that could describe unrelated videos.
- Video titles use FULL CAPS and more context than the short thumbnail headline:
  a concrete action/claim or an honest champion/role/session description. Mention
  a build only when it is meaningfully unusual or central to the video's value.
- Omit opening Spanish exclamation/question marks (`¡`, `¿`); closing `!`, `?`
  and `?!` are welcome when they fit. Keep accents and natural Spanish grammar.
- Approved title examples: NO SE PUEDE ESTAR MÁS FEDEADO!, CONOCES EL SHEN
  JUNGLA FULL AP?, SKARNER LETALIDAD ES UNA LOCURA!, PERO QUÉ ES ESTA
  TEMPORADA?!, FUNDAMENTOS DE LA JUNGLA, HE DESCUBIERTO EL NUEVO META,
  MI FIDDLESTICKS ES DEMASIADO BUENO, 15 KILLS!, ELISE JUNGLA ESTÁ DE VUELTA!,
  ME SAQUE UN FIZZ DE LA GALERA!
- Choose confident claims, unusual builds, questions, educational topics, exact
  stat hooks or champion comebacks according to the video's evidence. These
  examples teach packaging, not reusable claims or mandatory title formulas.
- Exact stats must be verified from the footage. Champion comeback claims need
  an evidenced return/buff premise; isolated damage numbers do not establish
  exceptional performance. Never leak production wording such as “final edit”
  or “final League” into the video title. Titles must be natural Spanish, not
  grammatical fragments or inflated versions of the thumbnail headline.
- Title inspiration: Thebausffs and Nemesis, with some Drututt directness. Prefer
  confident statements; questions are occasional, not the default.
- Contact-sheet captions show the candidate number and video title, not a repeat
  of the thumbnail headline.
- Use familiar gaming vocabulary. For League role, use **support**, not
  **apoyo/soporte**. Do not let this terminology preference dictate the video's subject.

## Evaluation

- Judge at mobile/feed size: recognizable face, clear subject, readable headline,
  compelling hook, and no distracting clutter.
- Choosing the best candidate is not publication approval. Report when all three
  need improvement and name the changes that would help.
- Specific-video judgments belong in that video's review notes. Generalize a
  lesson only when it is a reusable principle, not a particular champion or event.

## Additional channel feedback

## Approved visual-reference directions

- Baus-style close head crops: the expression fills much of its half of the
  image. Avoid treating a large shirt/chair/torso as a substitute for a big face.
  Smiles and genuine excitement are valuable, not just exaggerated shock.
- A champion can be a clean enlarged in-game silhouette staged on a recognizable
  Rift location, such as fountain spawn. It need not be a full splash background.
- Inventory/build layouts are useful when the actual build is the story. Use
  exact evidenced item assets; six icons are optional, not required decoration.
- Face plus a strong treated headline and close-perspective Rift environment is
  enough. No obligation to add a champion badge or item just because available.
- Relevant stat/passive/item elements may overlap the lower torso. Protect eyes,
  mouth, and the important background action; layering can use space efficiently.
- Cinematic, game-faithful character staging is welcome as illustration. Do not
  invent damage/stat values or present illustrated gameplay as evidence of a play.
- Start from ElmilloR's actual cutout, not an invented likeness or pose. The approved
  one-call workflow integrates it into the finished image; minor integration changes
  are acceptable, but verify identity, expression, inward gaze and game-asset fidelity.
- Typography and supporting effects can be generated. Verify Spanish spelling,
  headline completeness, and feed-size readability before publication.
