# Channel thumbnail style and guidelines

These principles apply across videos. Select the actual subject, hook, assets,
and expression from each video's evidence; do not reuse a previous video's
premise or treat candidate numbers as permanent editorial categories.

## Visual identity

- Large, recognizable ElmilloR reaction cutout, one clear game subject, and a
  big, short headline. Inspiration: Thebausffs and Drututt's bold, readable
  gaming-thumbnail approach, with ElmilloR's own identity.
- Keep the current enlarged reaction scale and white outline, including along
  straight cropped-photo edges. Faces and expressions must remain legible.
- Use strong foreground/background separation and restrained supporting color.
  Contrast should guide attention rather than make every element equally loud.
- Prefer ElmilloR on the right, flipped horizontally to face inward toward the
  left-hand headline/action. A left placement needs a specific composition reason
  and uses the unflipped source. Flipping is done in code, never by generating a face.

## Editorial judgment

- Package the video's strongest truthful reason to watch: surprise, tension,
  unusual choice, spectacular performance, humor, reversal, or a useful insight
  with a clear consequence. Do not force every video into the same hook.
- Prefer a concrete curiosity gap to generic advice or vague excitement. An
  educational hook can work when its value or surprising consequence is clear.
- Match the reaction to the actual emotion. Excitement, skepticism, confusion,
  amusement, and shock are alternatives, not a fixed mapping from content type.
- Inspect the actual available reaction photos and their catalog descriptions.
  Explain the photo's fit to each concept's evidence, headline and emotional promise.
  Closed-eye clenched-fist excitement is not the default for builds or questions.
- Prefer `smiling` more often for positive, confident, playful and normally exciting
  hooks. The clenched-fist pose is named `overly-hyped` and is a rare exception for
  genuinely extreme hype, not an ordinary win or merely a strong play. Its selection
  must explain the exceptional evidence and why smiling would undersell the moment.
- Experiment discipline: no additional paid image generations, revisions or broad
  evidence ingestion without the user requesting the next run. A cash budget alone
  is not permission for repeated image experiments; agree on the call count too.
- Three candidates should test different compelling interpretations of this
  video, not synonyms or mandatory action/champion/build slots.
- Preserve factual grounding. Never invent achievements, outcomes, items,
  characters, or stakes to strengthen the packaging.

## Composition and assets

- Choose gameplay, a character close-up/artwork, or another relevant background
  according to the hook. A background must help explain the subject or mood.
- In gameplay, make the important action identifiable at small size. Prefer a
  useful focal point over a wide screen full of unrelated HUD and effects.
- Use supporting item, rune, or character icons only when they add information.
  Avoid repeating a character badge when the main artwork already identifies it.
- Keep text and eyes unobstructed. Avoid accidental crops through facial features.
- Decorative shapes, outlines, and effects must improve hierarchy or separation;
  do not add them merely to make candidates look different.

## Copy and terminology

- Short, natural Spanish headlines; title and thumbnail should complement each
  other. Avoid literal sentence fragments and keyword soup.
- Use familiar gaming vocabulary. For the League role, use **support**, not
  **apoyo**. Do not let this terminology preference dictate the video's subject.

## Evaluation

- Judge at mobile/feed size: recognizable face, clear subject, readable headline,
  compelling hook, and no distracting clutter.
- Choosing the best candidate is not publication approval. Report when all three
  need improvement and name the changes that would help.
- Specific-video judgments belong in that video's review notes. Generalize a
  lesson only when it is a reusable principle, not a particular champion or event.

## Additional channel feedback

## Approved visual-reference directions

- Baus-style close head crops: the expression fills much of its half of the
  image. Avoid treating a large shirt/chair/torso as a substitute for a big face.
  Smiles and genuine excitement are valuable, not just exaggerated shock.
- A champion can be a clean enlarged in-game silhouette staged on a recognizable
  Rift location, such as fountain spawn. It need not be a full splash background.
- Inventory/build layouts are useful when the actual build is the story. Use
  exact evidenced item assets; six icons are optional, not required decoration.
- Face plus a strong treated headline and close-perspective Rift environment is
  enough. No obligation to add a champion badge or item just because available.
- Relevant stat/passive/item elements may overlap the lower torso. Protect eyes,
  mouth, and the important background action; layering can use space efficiently.
- Cinematic, game-faithful character staging is welcome as illustration. Do not
  invent damage/stat values or present illustrated gameplay as evidence of a play.
- Unlike the Tenshi reference's generated pose, use ElmilloR's original cutout.
  His face/body pixels must not be synthesized. Restore exact icons after artwork
  generation rather than trusting the model to redraw them perfectly.
- Typography and supporting effects can be generated. Verify Spanish spelling,
  headline completeness, and feed-size readability before publication.
